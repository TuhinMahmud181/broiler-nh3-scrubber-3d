Acid scrubbing catches 92% of a cold flock's ammonia, but only 36% of a hot flock's

A 25,000-bird broiler house exhausts through a bank of spray scrubbers fed with sulphuric acid. In hot weather the fans move far more air than the bank can take, and the excess bypasses untreated. What happens in each module

  1. Acid droplets from the PJ40 nozzles fall through the rising exhaust air. NH₃ crosses the gas film into the droplets; gas-side transfer controls the rate. Kya = 3916 · QL0.85 · D32−1.30 · C−0.21
  2. In the drop, ammonia meets acid at pH 3 and is protonated at once, so its back-pressure is nil: NH₃(aq) + H⁺ → NH₄⁺
  3. Overall reaction in the recirculating liquid: 2 NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄
  4. Plug flow of gas through the contact height z = 1.65 m gives the removal efficiency: η = 1 − exp(−Kya·z/G)
  5. A pH controller doses H₂SO₄ to hold pH 3. Ammonium builds up to 3 mol N/L (about 200 g/L ammonium sulphate), then a draw-off sends solution to the product tank and make-up water replaces it and the evaporation.
